# Pride for All: A Policy Brief on Disability & Rural Inclusion in Pride

A policy brief arguing that the inclusion gap at Indian Pride events, which
leaves disabled and rural LGBTQIA+ citizens behind, is primarily a failure of
state enforcement and information dissemination, not just of event organising.
Written as the policy-and-advocacy half of a two-person project; the brief
focuses on the extrinsic (state-facing) barriers and the enforcement actions
needed to close them.

## Context

Pride has scaled into a largely urban, English-language, physically
inaccessible event, which structurally excludes two constituencies:

- **Disabled LGBTQIA+ citizens**, for whom the last mile to a Pride venue,
  the absence of ISL interpretation, and inaccessible post-Pride venues are
  compounded by a double closet (the risk of losing family-provided
  housing, finances, and care if they come out).
- **Rural and Tier-2/3 citizens**, for whom the cost of travel, the digital
  divide, and an English-centric vocabulary of queerness make Pride
  functionally inaccessible, regardless of legal protections already in place.

The brief argues that closing this gap requires enforcing law that already
exists (the RPwD Act, 2016; the Navtej Singh Johar and NALSA judgments) and
using infrastructure the state already has (Doordarshan/All India Radio) —
not new legislation or new spending.

## Methodology

- Two anonymous interviews with disabled individuals about their experience
  of attempting to attend or participate in Pride.
- A site visit along the Delhi Pride route (Barakhamba Road → Tolstoy Road →
  Jantar Mantar), documenting accessibility barriers firsthand.

## Note on scope

This is an advocacy and policy-writing exercise, not a submitted or adopted
government proposal. The recommendations are grounded in the RPwD Act, 2016
and existing Supreme Court judgments.
